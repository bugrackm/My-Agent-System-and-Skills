# Playtest

The game's problems are invisible to its maker. Self-testing hides them: you
know the rules, you have practice, and you tune difficulty to your own skill,
which almost always means too hard.

## Method

- Start from the first grey-box build.
- Do not explain; watch. Do not rescue a stuck player; being stuck is design data.
- Ask them to think aloud. Ask "what are you trying to do?", not "do you like it?".
- 3-5 people per build is enough; several small rounds beat one large one.
- Record what they do more than what they say. Look for patterns across people.
  Fix the problem, not the solution they suggest.

## First-session checklist

1. Prepare a build that opens with one tap or one link and works without you.
   Try it on a real phone first.
2. Decide in advance what you want to learn (e.g. "do they get the controls in
   30 seconds?").
3. Find 3-5 people who are not you and not game developers. Friends are fine
   but polite; ask them to be honest.
4. Say nothing except "play".
5. Ask them to think aloud. Stay quiet; do not help, even when they struggle.
6. Note behavior: where they hesitate, die, laugh, quit, or do something
   unexpected. Screen-record if possible.
7. Afterwards ask open questions: "What was the goal?", "What was annoying?",
   "What would you change?"
8. Focus on problems two or more people hit.
9. Write findings into the decision log. Pick the top 1-3 fixes; the rest go to
   the list.
10. Fix, then test the next build with new people.

## What the findings decide

- Controls or first minute not understood → FTUE and feel tuning.
- Boring after 1-2 minutes → variety and difficulty curve.
- People replay with pleasure → polish and content.
